# Split domains across Name.com and iNET

The plan is to resell domains through two registrars. **Name.com's CORE API** becomes the primary for gTLDs (.com, .net, .org, .dev, .app, .io, .ai, .co, .xyz), with **Openprovider** as the fallback. **iNET** becomes the primary for .vn / .com.vn, with **P.A Vietnam** as the fallback. ResellerClub should be dropped. It is not just inconvenient. The founder could not get API access to it. Its sandbox has a live-credentials footgun. And the current module searches `.vn` through it, which is the wrong legal channel for Vietnamese registrants. Vietnamese law forces the split. A foreign registrar (or foreign reseller) **may only serve registrants located abroad**, and selling .vn without a registrar contract carries a **70–100M VND fine**. So .vn has to go through one of the 10 VNNIC-listed domestic registrars as a contracted distribution channel. For gTLDs, Name.com is the upstream that Vercel, Netlify, Bolt and Replit converged on. It has a modern OpenAPI REST API, a free sandbox, and "no fees, no application". In both tracks the user must be the registrant: never biveit. Every domain needs a verification step: ICANN email verification for gTLDs, and identity verification for .vn (digital signature, VNeID, or documents). The biggest unknowns are commercial, not technical. Nobody has yet confirmed that Name.com will onboard and fund a Vietnam-registered company. iNET's real API docs, sandbox and KYC upload path are unseen. And wholesale gTLD prices were not retrieved. Those are the first questions to put to sales.

## Two legal regimes force a two-registrar design

A single provider cannot cover both halves of the TLD list legally. For .vn, Decree 147/2024/ND-CP (in force for domains since 25/12/2024) sets the rules, and Circular 48/2025/TT-BKHCN (effective 10/02/2026) sets the procedures. Together they say that a foreign ICANN registrar **"chỉ được cung cấp dịch vụ cho chủ thể ở nước ngoài"**: it may serve only registrants abroad. A domestic registrar must be a Vietnamese enterprise, and it may build a distribution channel (kênh phân phối) only under a written contract with each channel member ([Decree 147 Ch.2 Sec.2](https://hethongphapluat.com/nghi-dinh-147-2024-nd-cp-quan-ly-cung-cap-su-dung-dich-vu-internet-va-thong-tin-tren-mang/chuong-2/muc-2)). VNNIC's registrar-policy page still cites Decree 15/2020 Art. 45.4(d): providing .vn services without being a registrar or holding a domestic agency contract is fined **70–100M VND** ([VNNIC](https://vnnic.vn/vi/ten-mien-vn/danh-cho-nha-dang-ky/chinh-sach-doi-voi-nha-dang-ky)). The foreign wholesalers confirm this in practice. WebNIC says "Vietnamese registrants must register through a local registrar" ([WebNIC KB](https://faq.webnic.cc/kb/vn/)). Netim says the same ([Netim](https://www.netim.com/domain-name/vn-domain.html)). CentralNic Reseller stopped new .vn registrations and transfers-in on **18 June 2026** ([CentralNic Reseller KB](https://kb.centralnicreseller.com/domains/tlds/vn)).

That puts NiceNIC, Openprovider and ResellerClub's .vn offerings off-limits for biveit's Vietnamese users. NiceNIC is actively recruiting Vietnamese resellers, but it is not on VNNIC's registrar list ([VNNIC list](https://www.vnnic.vn/vi/ten-mien-vn/danh-sach-nha-dang-ky-ten-mien)), so its .vn sales go through some upstream registrar. Using it for Vietnamese registrants looks non-compliant (inferred). Becoming a registrar is also out of scope: it means a VNNIC contract, in-country DNS on at least 2 clusters, and EPP integration ([Decree 147](https://hethongphapluat.com/nghi-dinh-147-2024-nd-cp-quan-ly-cung-cap-su-dung-dich-vu-internet-va-thong-tin-tren-mang/chuong-2/muc-2)).

For gTLDs the binding rules are ICANN's, and they flow to a reseller through its upstream contract. The RAA 2013 requires the registrant email to be verified within **15 days** of a registration, a transfer-in, or a registrant change. Otherwise the domain may be suspended ([ICANN](https://www.icann.org/en/blogs/details/the-2013-registrar-accreditation-agreement--what-you-need-to-know-13-2-2014-en)). The Registration Data Policy has been mandatory since **21 Aug 2025**. It drops the admin, tech and billing contacts. It also makes the Organization field, when it is filled, **the legal owner** of the domain ([INWX](https://www.inwx.de/en/blog/icann-rdp-update); [OpenSRS](https://opensrs.com/blog/what-icanns-updated-registration-data-policy-rdp-means-for-opensrs-resellers)).

There is one Vietnamese overlay on gTLDs too. Decree 147 requires Vietnamese holders of international domains to notify the authorities via thongbaotenmien.vn. Domestic sellers of international domains must report quarterly and run 2 DNS clusters in Vietnam ([Decree 147](https://hethongphapluat.com/nghi-dinh-147-2024-nd-cp-quan-ly-cung-cap-su-dung-dich-vu-internet-va-thong-tin-tren-mang/chuong-2/muc-2)). The notes do not settle whether biveit itself counts as a "domestic seller" when it resells a foreign registrar's .com. That is the single most important legal question to put to a Vietnamese lawyer (see open risks).

## Registrar picks: Name.com plus iNET, with named fallbacks

| TLDs | Primary | Fallback | Why primary | What is verified vs inferred |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| .com .net .org .dev .app .io .ai .co .xyz and other gTLDs | **Name.com CORE API** | **Openprovider REST `/v1`** | OpenAPI 3.1 REST, sandbox with preloaded credit, documented rate limits, "no fees, no application", and the upstream chosen by Vercel/Netlify/Bolt/Replit | **Verified:** API shape, sandbox, rate limits, partners. **Inferred/unknown:** whether a VN company is accepted and how it funds the balance (card vs wire), webhook payloads, wholesale prices |
| .vn .com.vn .id.vn .io.vn and other .vn second-level names | **iNET** (đại lý program) | **P.A Vietnam** | Public self-serve reseller program with "kết nối qua API" and a WHMCS module. 5M VND minimum first deposit; discounts up to 40%. VNNIC-listed and ICANN-accredited | **Verified:** program, deposit tiers, reseller policy. **Unknown:** API format, sandbox, how KYC uploads go through the API, price table (it did not render) |
| Dropped | ResellerClub, Cloudflare Registrar, Entri Sell, retail APIs (Porkbun, Namecheap, NameSilo, Dynadot) | — | See below | — |

**Name.com fits biveit's stack and audience best.** CORE API is "our latest version". It has a downloadable OpenAPI 3.1 spec. It offers official SDKs for TypeScript, Go and PHP, with Python "coming soon", and a "Reseller MVP Quickstart" that covers search, register and manage ([docs.name.com](https://docs.name.com/)). Its endpoints include `domains:checkAvailability`, a streaming `domains:searchStream`, register, details, DNS records, transfers, vanity nameservers and webhook notifications. Auth is HTTP Basic with a username and token. Limits are **20 req/s and 3,000 req/hr** account-wide, plus per-domain registration throttling with Retry-After ([Name.com API overview](https://docs.name.com/docs/api-overview)). The reseller page promises "No fees, no application, no sales call" and custom rate cards at volume ([Name.com Reseller](https://www.name.com/reseller)). Vercel moved to name.com as its upstream in September 2025 and sells "at registrar cost" ([Vercel changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-domains-at-cost-pricing-and-the-fastest-on-the-web)). Name.com's December 2025 release lists Vercel, Netlify, Bolt and Replit as API partners ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251209365505/en/name.com-API-Fuels-a-New-Era-of-Web-Building-with-Key-Partnerships)). That is biveit's exact peer group. Because the spec is OpenAPI, a Python client can be generated even before the official Python SDK ships.

**Openprovider is the right gTLD fallback rather than a co-primary.** Its REST `/v1` is equally clean: bearer token, OpenAPI, and register, check, transfer and renew endpoints ([developer.openprovider.com](https://developer.openprovider.com/)). The sandbox is self-signup at `cp.sandbox.openprovider.nl/signup`. The prepaid balance can be topped up by **card, PayPal or wire**, which is the most Vietnam-friendly funding path documented for any foreign candidate ([Openprovider blog](https://www.openprovider.com/blog/openprovider-costco)). Openprovider sells at cost, but behind an annual membership (sources disagree: from €49 or $49.99/yr), which only pays back at volume. If Name.com cannot onboard or fund a Vietnamese company, Openprovider becomes primary without any change to biveit's design. Note that `/v1beta` switches off on **30 June 2027**, so target `/v1` only ([Openprovider docs](https://docs.openprovider.com/doc/all)).

**iNET leads for .vn because it is the only domestic registrar with a transparent, self-serve reseller program.** iNET offers a referral tier and a white-label đại lý tier with "chiết khấu lên tới 40%". Deposit tiers run from under 20M to over 100M VND, and joining requires a company with a tax code ([iNET đại lý](https://inet.vn/dai-ly)). Its policy sets a **5M VND minimum first deposit** and promises API access. It refunds rejected .vn orders when someone registered the name first. And it says resellers **"must not register domains for customers using their own information"** ([iNET chính sách](https://inet.vn/dai-ly/chinh-sach)). The same policy has a lock-in clause to watch. If a reseller breaches its terms, it must notify holders 30 days ahead and unmigrated domains revert to iNET. iNET may also renew directly with customers if the reseller is unreachable. That is consumer-protective, but it means biveit does not fully own the relationship.

P.A Vietnam is the fallback. It is likely the largest .vn registrar. An open-source storefront shows a simple `daily.pavietnam.vn/interface.php` API: `username` + `apikey` + `action` parameters, an IP whitelist, and an API key that changes when the password changes. But the action names in that project were **guessed by its author**, and the real docs sit behind a login at kb.pavietnam.vn ([GitHub mientaymedia/api-pavietnam](https://github.com/mientaymedia/api-pavietnam)). Both iNET and PA are also ICANN-accredited. iNET's IANA ID 3234 and PA's IANA ID 1649 both come from secondary listings ([domaindetails](https://domaindetails.com/registrars/p-a-viet-nam-company-limited)).

**Steelman for one domestic registrar for everything.** Running gTLDs through iNET too would give one vendor, VND billing and no FX. It might also make the Vietnamese "domestic seller of international domains" reporting the registrar's problem rather than biveit's. That is a real advantage, and it is why the first sales call to iNET should ask about gTLD API coverage and pricing. Against it: iNET's API is unseen and probably legacy-style, and nothing confirms its .dev/.app/.ai coverage or its speed for search-as-you-type. Name.com is the proven choice for an AI site-builder. The routing design below makes this a configuration choice, not a rewrite, so it does not have to be decided up front.

**Why the others are dropped.** Cloudflare's Registrar API (beta April 2026; transfers and a sandbox added September 2026) sells at cost. But domains registered with Cloudflare **cannot change nameservers**, the registrant comes from the account's default contact, and nothing mentions resale ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/simplifying-domains/); [Cloudflare community](https://community.cloudflare.com/t/unable-to-replace-default-cloudflare-nameservers/861761)). Entri Sell is a commission/referral model: the user pays GoDaddy, IONOS or Squarespace, not biveit ([Entri docs](https://developers.entri.com/sell/overview.md)). It is only worth keeping as a zero-ops emergency option. The retail APIs are built for "your own account" and have no sub-account or white-label model. They also come with gates such as Namecheap's 20-domains-or-$50 eligibility and IPv4 whitelist, and Porkbun's monthly spend cap ([Porkbun](https://porkbun.com/llms/guides/register-a-domain)).

## ResellerClub: drop it and keep the module's shape

The existing `src/modules/domains` is already split the right way. `services/resellerclub.py` is a thin, product-agnostic client. `services/domains.py` holds product logic (`search_domains`, `register_domain`). Callers import the package. That structure survives. The vendor does not.

There are four reasons, in descending weight. First, the founder could not obtain API access from the WebPro Panel account. The likely explanation, which is inferred and not verified, is that WebPro is a sub-reseller panel under a parent ResellerClub reseller and does not expose its own API credentials. Becoming a direct ResellerClub reseller would mean starting over, so there is no sunk cost to protect. Second, the integration has a dangerous sandbox: the demo account is separate, and **live credentials on `test.httpapi.com` act on production**. A Blesta user reports an accidental real registration ([WHMCS docs](https://whmcsdocs.devops.cpanel.net/8-13/domains/domain-registrar-modules/resellerclub); [Blesta forum](https://www.blesta.com/forums/topic/3488-logicboxes-sandbox-not-working)). Third, its HTTP API (GET/POST with repeated query params, and an IP whitelist of up to 3 IPs that takes 30–60 minutes to propagate) is older in style than Name.com's or Openprovider's ([ResellerClub KB](https://manage.india.resellerclub.com/kb/node/3188)).

Fourth, and a concrete compliance bug: `DOMAIN_SEARCH_TLDS = ["com", "net", "org", "io", "co", "vn"]` in `src/constants.py` routes `.vn` through ResellerClub. ResellerClub is not on VNNIC's domestic list, so selling a Vietnamese user a .vn through it falls under Decree 147 Art. 13 and the fine above. The concrete step: replace `resellerclub.py` with `namecom.py` (gTLD client) and later `inet.py` (.vn client). Remove `vn` from the gTLD provider's routable TLDs. Delete the `resellerclub_*` settings in `src/config.py` and `.env.example`. These changes are still uncommitted, so they cost nothing to revert. ResellerClub is not worth keeping even as a tertiary fallback, because Openprovider fills that role with a better API and documented funding.

## One provider interface, an order state machine, and a reconciler

### The product flow, step by step

The user-facing flow follows the pattern every major builder converged on. Vercel, Netlify, Wix and Squarespace all make the **customer the registrant**, auto-create a platform-hosted DNS zone, provision TLS automatically, and auto-renew on the card on file after an email warning ([Netlify docs](https://docs.netlify.com/domains/configure-domains/register-and-buy-a-domain/); [Squarespace](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/articles/17131164996365); [Wix](https://www.wix.com/blog/does-wix-own-your-domain)).

1. **Search.** Use the provider's streaming check (Name.com's `searchStream`). Only "taken" results may be cached, and only briefly; an "available" result is advisory. Vercel credits its search speed to "layered caching, Bloom filters, and partitioned batching" ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-domains-at-cost-pricing-and-the-fastest-on-the-web)).
2. **Price.** Show first-year and renewal prices together. Use a per-TLD `min_years`, because **.ai requires a 2-year minimum** for both registration and renewal since Identity Digital took over ([Dynadot](https://www.dynadot.com/help/question/ai-domains-year-restrictions); [NameSilo](https://www.namesilo.com/blog/en/domain-name-search/ai-domain-registry-rules-pricing-and-renewals)).
3. **KYC.** For gTLDs, collect the registrant's name, email, phone and address, with a clearly labelled optional Organization field (filling it in makes that organization the legal owner). For .vn, collect the full Decree 147 Art. 9.7 set and an identity proof (see the compliance section).
4. **Pay.** Collect the money.
5. **Register.** First re-check availability and price live and uncached, then register on biveit's nameservers.
6. **Auto DNS/TLS.** Create the zone and issue the certificate **before** marking the domain active. .dev and .app are HSTS-preloaded at the TLD level, so they show a hard browser error until a valid certificate exists ([Porkbun KB](https://kb.porkbun.com/article/96-hsts-preload-and-google-registry)). Setting biveit's nameservers at registration also avoids the paid nameserver-change fee that CentralNic charges for .vn ([CentralNic KB](https://kb.centralnicreseller.com/domains/tlds/vn)). The equivalent fee at domestic registrars is unconfirmed.
7. **Renew.** biveit charges and renews from T-30, retrying until T-7. Send an email warning before charging (Squarespace uses 15 days). This step is needed because Shopify's community documents paid-but-not-renewed drift, and renewals failing when the card issuer blocks only the domain charge ([Shopify](https://community.shopify.com/t/domain-paid-but-still-pending-renewal/412026)).

### Backend design

The backend design is inference-level, but it follows from the documented failure modes and from the project's architecture conventions.

The domains module keeps its interface in `__init__.py`. Inside it:

- **Provider adapters** (`namecom.py`, `openprovider.py` later, `inet.py`). Each is a thin client with the same verb set: check, quote, register, renew, get, list, set nameservers, get auth code, lock/unlock, and balance.
- **A product service** (`domains.py`). It resolves the TLD to a provider through a **routing table in `constants.py`**: a primary and an ordered fallback list per TLD, gTLDs → `namecom` and `vn`/`com.vn` → `inet`. Wix runs exactly this kind of routing in production. Its registration agreement says it may send a registration to a different registrar "due to technical reasons" ([Wix–Tucows agreement](https://www.wix.com/domains/domain-registration-agreement/tucows)).
- **The stored record.** Each domain stores `provider` + `provider_ref` (the type-ID pattern), so later operations always go to the registrar that actually holds it. Failover only applies to new registrations.

**Orders and domains are separate entities.** An order runs `quoted → payment_pending → paid → registering → active`, with a `failed → refund_pending → refunded` branch. For .vn it also has `awaiting_identity`. A domain runs `pending_verification → active → expiring → expired/grace → redemption → deleted`, with side states `suspended_unverified` and `client_hold`. Under it sits an **append-only event log per domain**: every registrar request and response, payment and status observation. Following the table/stream duality, the log is the source of truth and the domain document is an index over it.

**Idempotency** rests on three moves. Mint a client order ID before any registrar call. On a timeout, **query the domain at the registrar before retrying the create**; "already ours" counts as success. Echo the quoted price on create, so that a premium flip or price change fails safely; Porkbun's API enforces this, and it also accepts an `Idempotency-Key` header ([Porkbun](https://porkbun.com/llms/guides/register-a-domain)). A nightly **reconciler** lists every domain at every provider account. It diffs expiry, status flags (clientHold, serverHold), nameservers and verification status against Mongo, and alerts on drift. It is the only defence against "paid but not renewed".

**Balance monitoring is a first-class job**, because every registration and renewal draws on a prepaid balance (Openprovider and iNET for certain; Name.com's funding model is unconfirmed). Poll balance after each order and on a schedule. Alert below a threshold of about one week of expected renewals plus a buffer. Never let a registrar-side auto-renew draw the balance down silently. The notes leave open whether to keep registrar auto-renew OFF (biveit renews explicitly) or ON as a safety net. Choose OFF with explicit biveit-side renewal: it keeps a single source of truth for who paid for what.

Security follows from the **Squarespace hijack of July 2024**. Migrated Google Domains accounts had MFA disabled, and attackers redirected DeFi front-ends to wallet drainers ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/dns-hijacks-target-crypto-platforms-registered-with-squarespace/)). Changing nameservers, DNS records or contacts, and requesting an auth code, should therefore require re-authentication and send an email to the registrant.

Finally, biveit must own support. A Vercel user stuck on clientHold was told by name.com that the domain was "partner-managed by Vercel" and it would not touch it ([Vercel community](https://community.vercel.com/t/my-domain-is-stuck-on-clienthold-name-com-confirmed-partner-managed-by-vercel/49981)). Self-serve auth code and unlock are therefore required, not nice-to-have.

## Compliance: the user owns the domain and proves who they are

**Registrant = user, always.** For gTLDs, putting biveit as registrant would make biveit the legal owner of every domain under the RDP Org-field rule. It would also breach the reseller's inherited RAA duties. For .vn, iNET's policy forbids it outright. It would also turn every future handover into a "change of holder", which under Circular 48/2025 needs a written instrument stating the domain's value ([LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/thong-tin/thong-tu-48-2025-tt-bkhcn-huong-dan-quan-ly-va-su-dung-tai-nguyen-internet-hieu-qua-422366-d1.html)).

**gTLD email verification.** The ICANN email verification is triggered on registration, on transfer-in, and on registrant data changes. It runs a **15-day clock per domain**, though the email only needs verifying once. Industry practice sends reminders on days 5, 10 and 13 ([ResellerClub](https://www.resellerclub.com/help/article/RAA-Email-Verification-and-Domain-Suspension-Rules); [OpenSRS](https://opensrs.com/blog/registrant-verification-announcement)). In the product, show a dashboard banner with a countdown and a `suspended_unverified` state that explains why the site broke. Find out from Name.com whether its verification email can carry biveit's branding (unknown).

**.vn identity is heavier.** Decree 147 Art. 9.7 requires these fields:

| Registrant | Required information |
|---|---|
| Individual | Full name, date of birth, personal ID / CCCD or passport number with place and date of issue, tax code, permanent and temporary address, phone, email |
| Organization | Enterprise code, tax code, head-office address, the domain manager and technical manager |

Art. 9.8 requires verification via **digital signature, the national e-ID system (VNeID), or equivalent technology** ([Decree 147](https://hethongphapluat.com/nghi-dinh-147-2024-nd-cp-quan-ly-cung-cap-su-dung-dich-vu-internet-va-thong-tin-tren-mang/chuong-2/muc-2)). Circular 48/2025 Art. 9 operationalises three methods: match a digital-signature certificate, match against the national e-ID system, or submit legally valid documents in person or by post. Data verified once can be reused for later registrations. The circular also sets the timing:

| Event | Deadline or consequence |
|---|---|
| Registrar notifies the result | Within **3 working days** of a valid application |
| Information is missing | 15-day window to update it |
| Not updated in time | 30 days of suspension, then revocation |

Filings use forms Mẫu 01a (organization), 01b (household business) and 01c (individual) ([LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/thong-tin/thong-tu-48-2025-tt-bkhcn-huong-dan-quan-ly-va-su-dung-tai-nguyen-internet-hieu-qua-422366-d1.html)). In practice, registrars collect this themselves. iNET wants a signed and stamped bản khai plus a copy of the CCCD from resellers ([iNET chính sách](https://inet.vn/dai-ly/chinh-sach)). TotHost has required eKYC with front and back photos of the CCCD since July 2024, though that post predates Circular 48 ([TotHost](https://tothost.vn/en/totnews-promotions/ekyc-instructions-when-registering-domains)).

The product therefore needs a reusable **verified-identity record per user**: one KYC, many domains. That record is personal data under Vietnam's PDP rules, so it needs explicit consent, minimal retention, and encrypted storage of ID images. The domain should sit in `awaiting_identity` until the registrar confirms verification. Unverified .vn domains are suspended and then revoked, and while suspended they **cannot change DNS or be transferred** ([Decree 147](https://hethongphapluat.com/nghi-dinh-147-2024-nd-cp-quan-ly-cung-cap-su-dung-dich-vu-internet-va-thong-tin-tren-mang/chuong-2/muc-2)).

**Finance.** .vn margins are thin and the invoice must split them. Under Circular 10/2025/TT-BTC:

| Name | Registration (one-off) | Maintenance (per year) |
|---|---|---|
| .vn | 100k VND | 350k VND |
| .com.vn | 100k VND | 250k VND |
| .id.vn | 10k VND | 50k VND |
| .io.vn | 10k VND | 20k VND |

([Luật Việt An](https://luatvietan.vn/quy-dinh-ve-phi-le-phi-su-dung-ten-mien-theo-thong-tu-10-2025-tt-btc.html); [VNNIC](https://vnnic.vn/vi/ten-mien-vn/danh-cho-nha-dang-ky/chinh-sach-doi-voi-nha-dang-ky))

P.A Vietnam sells year one of .vn at exactly the state fees (450k VND) and renews at 512k ([PA bảng giá](https://www.pavietnam.vn/vn/ten-mien-bang-gia.html)). iNET says resellers must sell .vn at the state-set fee, and that no VAT applies to the state components ([iNET](https://inet.vn/dai-ly/chinh-sach)). So .vn is a retention feature, not a profit centre. Invoices need separate lines for the state fees (no VAT) and the service fee (VAT applies). For gTLDs bought in USD and sold in VND, price renewals at renewal time with an FX buffer. The applicable VAT rate and any foreign-contractor tax are unconfirmed.

**Phase 0 (1–2 weeks).** Open the Name.com and Openprovider sandboxes and start the iNET sales conversation in parallel, since iNET is the long pole. Remove ResellerClub. Build the provider interface, the routing table, the order and domain state machines, and the event log against the Name.com sandbox.

**Phase 1.** Ship gTLDs on live Name.com: search, quote, checkout, register on biveit nameservers, auto DNS/TLS (TLS before active, for .dev/.app), the email-verification banner, biveit-side renewal, the reconciler, the balance alert, and self-serve auth code and unlock. Guide users to declare their international domain at thongbaotenmien.vn, or file it for them if counsel says biveit must.

**Phase 2.** Add .vn/.com.vn (and the cheap .id.vn and .io.vn) via iNET, with the reusable KYC record, the `awaiting_identity` state, and split invoicing.

**Phase 3.** Wire Openprovider as live failover for new gTLD registrations. Then consider transfers-in, which must respect the 60-day locks without hard-coding them. ICANN's Transfer Policy Review (47 recommendations approved by the GNSO in March 2025) will change those rules once implemented ([GNSO motion](https://lists.icann.org/hyperkitty/list/council@icann.org/message/ODZWZLJOGLHNAYX6BHGHRW5CD2ALSSVE/attachment/2/GNSOMotiontoapprovetheTransferPolicyReviewFinalReport.docx)).

**Open risks and unknowns**, in priority order:

1. **Will Name.com take a Vietnamese company, and how is it funded?** Unknown. No provider page states its reseller KYC or a country restriction. If the answer is no, Openprovider is promoted to primary.
2. **Is biveit, reselling a foreign registrar's gTLDs, a "domestic seller of international domains"** with quarterly reporting and in-country DNS duties under Decree 147? Unknown, and it needs legal counsel. If yes, routing gTLDs through iNET becomes much more attractive.
3. **What do iNET's real API, sandbox and KYC path look like?** Entirely unseen. It could force manual steps for .vn at launch.
4. **What does the full text of Circular 48/2025 say?** The forms and any channel-partner duties were not read (the copy found was truncated). Whether VNeID verification can be triggered by a reseller is unknown.
5. **Wholesale gTLD prices and AGP refund pass-through** are not retrieved for any provider.
6. **Is the 70–100M VND fine article still current?** Decree 15/2020 has been amended since; VNNIC still cites the article.
7. **ERRP reminder and parking duties, abuse-handling duties (RAA §3.18 and the 2024 DNS Abuse amendments), and VAT/e-invoice treatment** are all unverified from primary sources.

## Conclusion

The structure of this decision is set by law, not by API quality. Vietnamese registrants cannot legally get .vn from a foreign registrar, and every gTLD reseller inherits ICANN's verification and ownership rules. So the "multiple registrars" question is not about redundancy. Two registrars are the minimum compliant shape. The only real choice is whether gTLDs also go domestic. That choice turns on one unanswered legal question about thongbaotenmien reporting, and on whether Name.com will fund a Vietnamese account. The design handles both answers, because routing lives in a table rather than in code.

The underrated work is not registration but everything after it: reconciliation, balance alerts, verification countdowns, and support for clientHold. Every public failure in the notes, at Shopify, Vercel, Wix and Squarespace, happened in the lifecycle, not at checkout. For .vn the economics are near zero margin and the KYC burden is high. That makes .vn a retention feature that should reuse one verified-identity record across a user's domains, rather than a revenue line worth optimising.
